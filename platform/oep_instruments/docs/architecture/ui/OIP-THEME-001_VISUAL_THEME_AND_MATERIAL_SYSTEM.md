# 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual material system used throughout the OEP Instruments platform.

It establishes a unified appearance for every instrument while allowing each instrument to maintain its own operational identity.

The material system governs appearance only.

Engineering behavior is defined elsewhere.

---

# 2. Philosophy

Professional engineering instruments are designed to disappear into the workflow.

Their appearance inspires confidence.

They are durable.

Predictable.

Readable.

The OEP material system shall communicate the same qualities.

---

# 3. Design Objectives

The visual system shall appear:

Professional

Industrial

Functional

Modern

Durable

Premium

Timeless

Purpose-built

The design shall avoid trends that quickly become dated.

---

# 4. Material Language

Primary materials shall emulate:

Injection-molded engineering plastics

Rubber overmold grips

Powder-coated metals

Anodized aluminum

Tempered glass

Matte LCD bezels

Soft-touch buttons

Materials shall appear realistic rather than stylized.

---

# 5. Surface Finish

Preferred finishes:

Matte

Fine texture

Low reflectivity

Anti-glare

Soft edge highlights

Avoid:

High gloss

Chrome

Mirror reflections

Decorative gradients

---

# 6. Instrument Body

The instrument housing shall communicate:

Durability

Precision

Mechanical strength

Professional quality

Body geometry shall prioritize ergonomics over appearance.

---

# 7. Display Bezel

Displays shall include:

Slight recessed mounting

Consistent bezel thickness

Minimal reflections

Soft shadowing

Professional labeling

Display bezels shall remain visually secondary to the display itself.

---

# 8. Button Materials

Buttons shall resemble molded elastomer or engineering polymer.

Characteristics:

Matte finish

Subtle edge highlights

Visible travel

Consistent spacing

Comfortable touch targets

---

# 9. Rotary Selector Materials

Rotary selectors shall resemble physical mechanical switches.

Characteristics:

Knurled edge

Center cap

Index marker

Mechanical shadow

Visible detents

Distinct active position

---

# 10. Color Palette

Primary colors:

Instrument Black

Charcoal Gray

Engineering White

Safety Red

Safety Yellow

Status Green

Information Blue

Accent colors shall be limited and purposeful.

---

# 11. Lighting

Lighting shall simulate:

Ambient light

Display illumination

Button highlights

Soft material reflections

Lighting shall remain subtle.

---

# 12. Shadows

Shadows communicate depth.

Requirements:

Soft

Consistent

Minimal

Realistic

No exaggerated elevation.

---

# 13. Borders

Borders shall define:

Displays

Controls

Panels

Dialogs

Borders shall remain thin and understated.

---

# 14. Corners

Corner radii shall remain consistent across the platform.

Sharp corners shall be reserved for engineering displays and tables.

Rounded corners shall reflect molded hardware.

---

# 15. Spacing

Spacing shall communicate hierarchy.

Primary controls receive the greatest emphasis.

Related controls remain visually grouped.

White space improves readability.

---

# 16. Branding

Brand placement shall be limited.

Branding shall never dominate the instrument.

The engineer's attention belongs on engineering information.

---

# 17. Display Illumination

Displays shall support:

Normal brightness

Night mode

Outdoor visibility

High contrast

Uniform illumination

Brightness shall remain even across the display.

---

# 18. Environmental Appearance

The visual system shall remain usable in:

Bright sunlight

Dark environments

Garages

Industrial facilities

Field service

Educational laboratories

Appearance shall reinforce usability under all conditions.

---

# 19. Platform Consistency

Every platform shall preserve the same material language.

Android

Windows

Linux

Future iOS

Dedicated handheld hardware

The user should immediately recognize an OEP Instrument.

---

# 20. Premium Quality

Every rendered surface shall meet commercial-quality standards suitable for a premium engineering product.

Visual quality shall reinforce confidence in the instrument.

---

# 21. Extensibility

Future instruments inherit this material system automatically.

New materials shall expand the system without replacing existing conventions.

---

# 22. Future Hardware

The material system shall transition naturally to physical hardware products.

Digital appearance shall prepare users for future dedicated OEP instruments.

---

# 23. Core Principles

1.

Professional engineering equipment is the design reference.

2.

Materials communicate quality.

3.

Function dictates appearance.

4.

Visual restraint builds confidence.

5.

Consistency outweighs novelty.

6.

Every instrument belongs to one product family.

7.

The interface should resemble manufactured equipment.

8.

Future hardware shall inherit this visual language directly.
