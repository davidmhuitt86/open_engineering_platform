# 1. Purpose

This document defines the visual design language for every instrument within the OEP Instruments platform.

Its purpose is to ensure every current and future instrument appears to belong to one professional product family regardless of platform.

This document governs appearance only.

Engineering behavior is defined elsewhere.

---

# 2. Philosophy

The objective is not to imitate consumer mobile applications.

The objective is to create professional engineering instruments.

Every screen should appear purpose-built.

Every interaction should reinforce confidence.

The interface shall disappear behind the engineering task.

---

# 3. Design Goals

The design system shall be:

Professional

Timeless

Readable

Minimal

Predictable

Consistent

Responsive

High Contrast

Platform Independent

---

# 4. Product Family

Every instrument shall be immediately recognizable as an OEP Instrument.

Shared characteristics include:

Typography

Spacing

Control geometry

Animation

Status indicators

Iconography

Sound

Haptics

Dialogs

Settings

Navigation

---

# 5. Visual Hierarchy

Information priority:

Primary Measurement

↓

Instrument Mode

↓

Measurement Units

↓

Status Indicators

↓

Secondary Measurements

↓

Session Information

↓

Diagnostics

↓

Configuration

Nothing shall compete visually with the primary measurement.

---

# 6. Display Philosophy

Displays shall emphasize engineering readability.

Requirements:

Large measurement digits

High contrast

Stable layout

Minimal animation

No decorative gradients

No visual noise

---

# 7. Materials

Visual materials shall resemble professional equipment.

Preferred materials:

Matte polymer

Anodized aluminum

Tempered glass

Rubber overmold

Anti-glare displays

Avoid glossy consumer styling.

---

# 8. Color System

Primary colors communicate engineering state.

Green

Operational

Blue

Information

Amber

Warning

Red

Critical

Gray

Inactive

Color shall communicate state rather than decoration.

---

# 9. Typography

Typography shall prioritize readability.

Requirements:

Large numerals

Clear engineering symbols

Consistent alignment

Monospaced measurement digits

Stable character widths

No decorative fonts.

---

# 10. Icons

Icons shall be:

Simple

Recognizable

Engineering focused

Consistent

Scalable

Text labels shall accompany icons where ambiguity exists.

---

# 11. LCD Display Language

Measurement displays shall resemble professional engineering instruments.

Characteristics:

Seven-segment numerals where appropriate

Engineering annunciators

Measurement units

Stable brightness

Minimal transition effects

Consistent spacing

---

# 12. Controls

Control styles:

Rotary selector

Push button

Soft key

Toggle

Navigation pad

Virtual controls shall resemble physical controls.

---

# 13. Rotary Switches

Characteristics:

Mechanical detents

Shadow depth

Clear active position

Rotational animation

Audible click

Haptic detent

Consistent angular spacing

---

# 14. Buttons

Buttons shall provide:

Visual depression

Immediate response

Consistent size

Readable labels

Haptic confirmation

Minimal animation

---

# 15. Animation

Animations communicate engineering state.

Examples:

Power on

Connection

Measurement update

Streaming

Probe movement

No animation shall exist purely for decoration.

---

# 16. Sound Design

Sound shall reinforce interaction.

Examples:

Button click

Rotary detent

Continuity beep

Alert

Connection

Completion

Every sound shall have purpose.

---

# 17. Haptic Design

Where supported:

Rotary detents

Button presses

Continuity

Warnings

Connection established

Critical alerts

Haptics reinforce physical interaction.

---

# 18. Layout System

Layouts shall support:

Phone

Tablet

Landscape

Portrait

Foldable devices

Future handheld hardware

Scaling shall preserve proportions.

---

# 19. Accessibility

Support:

Large text

High contrast

Color-blind indicators

Screen readers

External keyboards

Stylus

Accessibility shall never reduce engineering clarity.

---

# 20. Branding

Branding shall remain subtle.

The instrument remains the visual focus.

Branding shall never reduce usable display area.

---

# 21. Premium Standard

Every instrument shall meet commercial quality standards suitable for sale as a premium engineering application.

Interfaces shall feel equivalent to dedicated professional equipment.

---

# 22. Cross-Instrument Consistency

Users familiar with one OEP Instrument shall immediately understand another.

Shared interaction patterns include:

Settings

Navigation

Dialogs

Status indicators

Connection indicators

Measurement history

Bookmarks

Notifications

---

# 23. Platform Independence

The design language shall remain consistent across:

Android

Windows

Linux

Future iOS

Dedicated handheld hardware

Platform conventions shall never compromise the product identity.

---

# 24. Future Evolution

The design system shall accommodate future instruments without requiring redesign.

Future instruments inherit this document as their visual foundation.

---

# 25. Core Principles

1.

Engineering clarity comes before visual style.

2.

Professional equipment is the design reference.

3.

Every interaction reinforces confidence.

4.

Visual consistency builds familiarity.

5.

Controls shall resemble physical instruments.

6.

Motion communicates state.

7.

The engineer uses an instrument, not an app.

8.

Every instrument belongs to one professional family.
