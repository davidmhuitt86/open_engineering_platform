# 1. Purpose

This document defines the color architecture used throughout the OEP Instruments platform.

Color shall communicate engineering information.

Color shall never exist solely for decoration.

The color system establishes a consistent visual language across every instrument.

---

# 2. Philosophy

Professional engineering equipment uses color sparingly.

Every color conveys meaning.

Users shall never need to guess what a color represents.

Color shall reinforce engineering awareness rather than visual appeal.

---

# 3. Objectives

The color system shall be:

Professional

Consistent

Accessible

Minimal

Purposeful

Platform Independent

Timeless

---

# 4. Design Principles

Colors communicate:

State

Priority

Safety

Interaction

Status

Warnings

Critical Conditions

Colors shall never communicate engineering values.

---

# 5. Neutral Palette

Neutral colors define the instrument.

Examples:

Instrument Black

Charcoal

Dark Graphite

Engineering Gray

Light Gray

White

Neutral colors establish the visual foundation.

---

# 6. Status Colors

Status colors shall remain identical across every instrument.

Operational

Green

Information

Blue

Warning

Amber

Critical

Red

Inactive

Gray

Disconnected

Dark Gray

---

# 7. Measurement Values

Measurements shall normally remain monochrome.

Measurements shall never change color merely because their value changes.

Color changes shall indicate engineering state only.

---

# 8. Background Colors

Backgrounds shall remain visually quiet.

Preferred:

Near Black

Dark Gray

Off White

Low Reflection Gray

Backgrounds shall maximize measurement readability.

---

# 9. Display Colors

Display colors shall emphasize:

Measurements

Units

Annunciators

Warnings

Connection Status

Streaming Status

Background colors shall never compete with displayed information.

---

# 10. Warning Colors

Warnings shall use:

Amber

Examples:

Probe Missing

Measurement Invalid

Simulation Paused

Connection Weak

Warning colors shall remain reserved for warning conditions.

---

# 11. Critical Colors

Critical conditions use:

Red

Examples:

Over Voltage

Over Current

Host Lost

Authentication Failure

Transport Failure

Critical colors shall remain highly visible.

---

# 12. Success Colors

Success states use:

Green

Examples:

Connected

Measurement Complete

Calibration Successful

Simulation Ready

Green shall never indicate measurement magnitude.

---

# 13. Informational Colors

Informational events use:

Blue

Examples:

Streaming

Playback

Selection

Synchronization

Configuration

Blue shall never indicate urgency.

---

# 14. Disabled State

Disabled controls shall use subdued neutral colors.

Disabled elements shall remain recognizable while clearly indicating inactivity.

---

# 15. Selection Colors

Selections shall remain highly visible.

Selection colors shall:

Avoid conflict with warning colors

Avoid conflict with critical colors

Remain visible under all themes

---

# 16. Graph Colors

Engineering graphs shall use color to distinguish objects.

Color shall never replace labels.

Graphs shall remain understandable in monochrome.

---

# 17. Accessibility

The color system shall support:

Color Blind Safe Palette

High Contrast Mode

Low Vision Mode

Monochrome Operation

No engineering information shall depend solely upon color.

---

# 18. Theme Support

Themes may alter:

Background

Surface

Accent

Highlight

Borders

Themes shall never alter engineering meaning.

---

# 19. Ambient Lighting

Colors shall remain readable under:

Bright Sunlight

Indoor Lighting

Night Operation

Industrial Facilities

Vehicle Interiors

Field Service

---

# 20. Future Displays

The color system shall support:

LCD

OLED

MicroLED

E-Ink

Industrial Displays

Future display technologies

---

# 21. Branding

Brand colors shall remain secondary.

Engineering information shall always dominate.

Brand identity shall never reduce usability.

---

# 22. Extensibility

Future instruments shall inherit the same color language.

Additional colors shall preserve existing engineering meanings.

Existing meanings shall never be reassigned.

---

# 23. Core Principles

1.

Color communicates engineering state.

2.

Measurements remain visually neutral.

3.

Warning colors remain reserved.

4.

Critical colors remain unmistakable.

5.

Accessibility is mandatory.

6.

Consistency builds trust.

7.

Engineering clarity overrides visual style.

8.

Every instrument shares one professional color language.
