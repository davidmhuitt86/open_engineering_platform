# 1. Purpose

This document establishes the industrial design language, interaction philosophy, and user experience standards for every instrument within the OEP Instruments platform.

Every current and future instrument shall conform to this document.

This document is independent of:

• Android

• Windows

• Linux

• Future embedded hardware

The instrument shall remain recognizable regardless of platform.

---

# 2. Design Philosophy

OEP Instruments shall emulate professional engineering equipment rather than conventional mobile applications.

The objective is not to create attractive software.

The objective is to create believable engineering instruments.

The engineer shall feel they are operating professional equipment.

---

# 3. Guiding Principles

Every instrument shall be:

Professional

Predictable

Responsive

Minimal

Readable

Purpose-built

Engineering focused

Marketing aesthetics shall never supersede usability.

---

# 4. Authenticity

Every instrument shall behave consistently with its real-world counterpart.

Examples include:

Power-on sequence

LCD behavior

Button operation

Rotary selector behavior

Range switching

Measurement hold

Auto-ranging

Manual ranging

Instrument alerts

Calibration screens

Battery status

The objective is familiarity for practicing engineers.

---

# 5. Visual Identity

Every instrument shall share a common industrial design language.

Common elements include:

Typography

Color palette

Status indicators

Button styling

Display styling

Animations

Dialogs

Menus

Icons

Branding

Individual instruments may differ in layout while remaining recognizably OEP.

---

# 6. Display Philosophy

Displays shall prioritize readability over decoration.

Requirements:

Large primary values

High contrast

Minimal clutter

Consistent spacing

Stable layout

No unnecessary animation

No decorative effects that reduce readability.

---

# 7. Controls

Controls shall resemble professional engineering equipment.

Examples:

Rotary switches

Momentary push buttons

Soft keys

Navigation keys

Function buttons

Dedicated measurement buttons

No hidden gesture-only functionality for primary operations.

---

# 8. Rotary Selectors

Rotary selectors shall include:

Mechanical detents

Audible click

Haptic feedback (where available)

Clear active position

Smooth animation

Consistent rotational direction

Rotation shall feel deliberate rather than decorative.

---

# 9. Buttons

Buttons shall provide:

Visual press feedback

Haptic feedback (where available)

Immediate response

Consistent spacing

Consistent sizing

Professional labeling

Animations shall reinforce operation rather than entertain.

---

# 10. Startup Sequence

Every instrument performs a startup sequence.

Examples:

Display segment test

Firmware version

Instrument model

Calibration verification

Battery status

Connection status

Ready

Startup duration shall remain brief while reinforcing realism.

---

# 11. Sound

Sound effects shall be minimal.

Examples:

Button click

Rotary detent

Continuity beep

Connection established

Fault alert

Measurement complete

No unnecessary sounds.

All sounds shall be disableable.

---

# 12. Haptic Feedback

Where supported:

Rotary detents

Button presses

Continuity

Connection

Alerts

Critical warnings

Haptics reinforce physical interaction.

---

# 13. Color Philosophy

Color communicates engineering state.

Green

Operational

Blue

Information

Yellow

Warning

Red

Critical

Gray

Inactive

Color shall never be decorative.

---

# 14. Motion

Animations shall communicate state.

Examples:

Power on

Probe movement

Measurement update

Instrument switching

Connection

Streaming

No decorative animation loops.

---

# 15. Touch Interaction

Touch targets shall support:

Gloved operation

Field conditions

Outdoor visibility

Fast operation

One-handed operation

Precision stylus operation

Professional usability takes priority over compactness.

---

# 16. Information Hierarchy

Priority:

Primary Measurement

↓

Measurement Mode

↓

Range

↓

Connection

↓

Session

↓

Status

↓

Secondary Information

The engineer shall never search for the current measurement.

---

# 17. Branding

Branding shall remain understated.

The instrument itself remains the focus.

Branding shall never reduce usable display area.

---

# 18. Accessibility

Support:

Large text

Color-blind indicators

Screen readers

External keyboards

Landscape

Portrait

High contrast

Accessibility shall not alter engineering accuracy.

---

# 19. Future Hardware

The same user interface shall be deployable to:

Android

Windows

Linux

Dedicated handheld devices

Future Divad hardware

The industrial design language shall survive platform changes.

---

# 20. Premium Quality Standard

Every instrument shall meet a professional engineering quality standard suitable for commercial sale.

The user experience shall be comparable to dedicated engineering equipment in terms of clarity, responsiveness, and reliability.

Every interaction shall reinforce confidence in the instrument.

---

# 21. Core Principles

1.

The engineer is using an instrument.

Not an app.

2.

Engineering clarity overrides visual decoration.

3.

Authenticity builds confidence.

4.

Controls shall behave predictably.

5.

Displays prioritize readability.

6.

Motion communicates state.

7.

Consistency outweighs novelty.

8.

Every instrument belongs to one professional family.
